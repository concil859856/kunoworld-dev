# Measured render cost, 2026-09-15 (first GPU runs)

The first real GPU runs of all four serving profiles. Until now every speed in [costs.md](costs.md) was an estimate;
these are measurements, taken from the workers' own signed receipts (`gpu_seconds` counts all four GPUs for H3) and
cross-checked against the smoke test's wall-clock timings. Raw records: `/video/data/gpu-tests/`.

**Read these as one data point per profile, not a benchmark.** Single runs, one clip length each, weights already in
page cache, and **no confidential-computing mode** (CC costs 15-25% more compute, per `research_tee.md` 2.5).
`kuno-bench` still has to measure the resolution, duration and fps grid.

## What was measured

| Profile | Hardware | Output | GPU time | GPU-seconds per output second |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `ltx-2.5-fast` | 1x RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell 96 GB | 2.0 s, 1280x704, 24 fps | 9.51 s | **4.76** |
| `ltx-2.5-pro` | 1x RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell 96 GB | 2.0 s, 1280x704, 24 fps | 105.9 s | **53.0** |
| `h3` | 4x H200 141 GB | 5.17 s, 1344x768, 24 fps | 321.9 s | **62.3** |
| `h3-reference` | 4x H200 141 GB | 5.17 s, 1344x768, 24 fps | 523.2 s | **101.3** |

Fixed per-job work (VAE decode, the safety check, encoding, sealing) is inside these numbers, so short clips look
dearer per second than long ones will. The LTX clips are only 2 s; expect their per-second cost to fall at 5-10 s.

## Cost per second of finished video

GPU-hour prices: RTX PRO 6000 $2.19 (MassedCompute, what we rented) and $1.879 with AMD SEV-SNP confidential
computing (Verda); H200 $4.00 (our 8-GPU Tokyo box at $32/h) and $4.80 with Intel TDX confidential computing (Phala).
See [research_cc_gpu_rental.md](../research_cc_gpu_rental.md).

| Profile | Plain rental | Confidential rental | Confidential at 60% utilization | Private price today | Margin at 60% |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `ltx-2.5-fast` | $0.0029 | $0.0025 | $0.0041 | $0.05 | **12x** |
| `ltx-2.5-pro` | $0.0322 | $0.0276 | $0.0461 | $0.075 | **1.6x** |
| `h3` | $0.0692 | $0.0831 | $0.1385 | $0.20 | **1.4x** |
| `h3-reference` | $0.1125 | $0.1350 | $0.2251 | $0.30 | **1.3x** |

Standard-mode prices (LTX only) leave less: `ltx-2.5-fast` $0.04/s is 9.7x cost, `ltx-2.5-pro` $0.055/s is 1.2x.

## What this says about the placeholders

1. **The price ladder is the wrong shape.** `ltx-2.5-fast` carries a 12x margin while `h3-reference` carries 1.3x.
   Either the fast profile is far too dear for what it uses, or the H3 profiles are too cheap for theirs.
2. **The VCU weights understate `ltx-2.5-pro` badly.** Normalizing measured cost to the placeholder's `fast = 3`:

   | Profile | Placeholder VCU/s | Measured, cost-normalized |
   |---|---|---|
   | `ltx-2.5-fast` | 3 | 3 |
   | `ltx-2.5-pro` | 9 | **33** |
   | `h3` | 60 | **100** |
   | `h3-reference` | 90 | **163** |

   A pro second costs about 11x a fast second, not 3x: the distilled model runs 8 steps in two stages, the dev model
   30 steps plus 3, with guidance passes on top. Miners paid by these weights would lose money on `ltx-2.5-pro`.
3. **Confidential H200 capacity is dear relative to H3's price.** At $4.80/GPU-h and 60% utilization, `h3-reference`
   costs $0.225 per output second against a $0.30 price, before platform costs, storage or refunds.

## Suggested next steps

1. Re-measure with `kuno-bench` on one rented RTX PRO 6000 (about $5) across durations 2-10 s and both resolutions,
   then run `kuno-devkit derive-rates` for a full proposal instead of these four points.
2. Re-run one H3 job at 10 s to see how much of its cost is fixed per job.
3. Only then set prices, and make the margin deliberate per profile rather than an accident of the placeholders.
4. Measure the confidential-computing overhead for diffusion when we first get a confidential GPU; today's 15-25% is
   an LLM number.
